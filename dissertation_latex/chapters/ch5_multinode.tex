\section{Experimental Setup Recap}
\label{sec:mn-setup}
The four layouts run at four sizes in 2D and 3D over the node counts admitted by the
unknowns-per-core window: up to 8 nodes at 5.0M, 16 at 10.0M, 32 at 20.25M, and 64 at
41.0M. All runs use CG+GAMG; iteration counts stay at 11--12 across sizes and node counts.

\section{2D Results}
\label{sec:2d}
$32\times4$ had the lowest median runtime at every size and at almost every node count.
For 41.0M unknowns it was faster than $128\times1$ by 11.3\% on one node
($105.0\,\text{s}$ against $118.4\,\text{s}$), 11.7\% on 16 nodes, and 12.0\% on 64 nodes.
$64\times2$ was a few percent behind $32\times4$.

From one node to two, speedup was 0.93--0.99 for every layout and size. From two nodes on,
each doubling gave speedups near 2.0 (2.00, 1.99, 2.00, 2.01 for $32\times4$ at 41.0M).
Efficiency relative to one node settled at 45--48\%.

At scale, $16\times8$ reached 33\% efficiency at 20.25M on 32 nodes ($32\times4$: 48\%) and
18\% at 41.0M on 64 nodes, with runtime $9.19\,\text{s}$ against $4.03\,\text{s}$ for
$32\times4$.

\section{3D Results}
\label{sec:3d}
The layout order matched 2D ($32\times4$ fastest, $128\times1$ slowest of the four,
$16\times8$ weakest at scale), with two differences. From one node to two, two nodes were
faster than one (speedup 1.02--1.10); efficiency at two nodes was 51--55\% (2D: 47--49\%).
The 3D seven-point stencil exchanges a larger halo relative to subdomain interior than the
2D five-point stencil. At 41.0M on 64 nodes, $16\times8$ kept 35\% efficiency (2D: 18\%)
and was 11\% slower than $32\times4$ (2D: 128\% slower).
